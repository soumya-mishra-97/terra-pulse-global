# Crude birth rate and crude death rate

The crude birth rate (SP.DYN.CBRT.IN) is the number of live births occurring during the year per 1,000 people estimated at midyear. The crude death rate (SP.DYN.CDRT.IN) is the number of deaths occurring during the year per 1,000 people estimated at midyear. Subtracting the crude death rate from the crude birth rate gives the rate of natural increase per 1,000 people, which excludes migration.

Crude rates depend strongly on age structure. A country with an older population can have a higher crude death rate than a poorer country with a younger population, even if age-specific mortality is lower. Crude rates should therefore not be used alone to compare health outcomes.

# Total fertility rate

The total fertility rate (SP.DYN.TFRT.IN) is the number of children that would be born to a woman if she were to live to the end of her childbearing years and bear children in accordance with the age-specific fertility rates of the specified year. A total fertility rate of about 2.1 births per woman is commonly described as replacement-level fertility in low-mortality settings; the exact replacement level is higher where child mortality is higher.

# Life expectancy at birth

Life expectancy at birth (SP.DYN.LE00.IN) is the number of years a newborn infant would live if prevailing patterns of mortality at the time of its birth were to stay the same throughout its life. It is a summary of current mortality conditions, not a forecast of how long any cohort will actually live. Short-term shocks such as epidemics can lower period life expectancy sharply and temporarily.
