# Demographic transition

The demographic transition describes the historical shift from high birth and death rates to low birth and death rates. Mortality usually declines first, so populations grow quickly during the transition; fertility declines later, and growth slows. Countries are at different stages, which is why growth rates and age structures differ widely.

# Population momentum

A population with a large share of young people can keep growing for decades even after fertility falls to replacement level, because many people are entering childbearing ages. Conversely, a population with below-replacement fertility may continue to grow for some time because of momentum or immigration before eventually declining.

# Reading growth figures carefully

A high percentage growth rate in a small population can add fewer people than a modest rate in a very large population. When asking which countries contribute most to world population growth, compare absolute changes in population as well as percentage rates. Growth is the combined effect of natural increase (births minus deaths) and net migration.
