# Three different kinds of number

An observed count is the result of an enumeration such as a census at a specific date. An estimate is a modelled value for a past or current date that combines censuses, surveys, vital registration and demographic models. A projection is a forward-looking scenario that depends on explicit assumptions about future fertility, mortality and migration.

No organisation counts every person on Earth in real time. Global population figures are estimates with a reference date, and they are revised as new censuses and surveys become available.

# Uncertainty

The World Development Indicators publish point estimates without confidence or prediction intervals. TerraPulse therefore states that uncertainty is not quantified by the source rather than inventing intervals. Estimates for countries without a recent census or with incomplete vital registration are less certain than the point values suggest.

# The illustrative counter

The animated world-population counter on the TerraPulse dashboard is an illustrative projection, not an official figure. It starts from the latest source estimate and applies the source's latest annual exponential growth rate continuously from the estimate's reference date. Real population change varies over time, so the counter's value drifts away from what an official estimate would show. TerraPulse labels it accordingly wherever it appears.

# Projections in TerraPulse

TerraPulse has not imported an official projection dataset (such as the UN World Population Prospects projection variants). Reports therefore mark future-projection sections as data unavailable rather than extrapolating trends.
